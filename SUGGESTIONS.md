**Introduction**

Aquaculture has become a vital contributor to global food security due to the rising demand for seafood and the decline of natural fisheries. Among various aquaculture sectors, shrimp farming is one of the most economically important industries worldwide. However, intensive shrimp aquaculture often results in significant environmental challenges including high energy consumption, nutrient pollution, greenhouse gas emissions, and degradation of coastal ecosystems.

To address these sustainability concerns, Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture (IMTA) has gained attention as an environmentally friendly approach. IMTA integrates species from different trophic levels within a single farming system so that waste produced by one species becomes a resource for another. In shrimp–seaweed IMTA systems, seaweed utilizes dissolved nutrients and carbon dioxide released from shrimp ponds, thereby improving water quality and reducing environmental impacts.

In addition, carbon emissions from aquaculture systems are increasingly being examined in the context of climate change mitigation. Seaweeds have the ability to capture carbon through photosynthesis, making them an effective natural carbon sink. Therefore, integrating seaweed cultivation with shrimp farming may help reduce the overall carbon footprint of aquaculture operations.

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) is widely used to evaluate the environmental impacts associated with a product or process throughout its life cycle. Applying LCA to aquaculture systems allows researchers to identify major emission sources and evaluate sustainability improvements achieved through integrated farming systems.

The **total CO₂ emission in the aquaculture system** can be modeled as the sum of emissions from different sources.

**Total CO₂ Emission**

CO2total=CO2feed+CO2energy+CO2respiration+CO2decomposition

Where:

* CO2feed\= emissions from feed production

* CO2energy\= emissions from electricity consumption

* CO2respiration\= shrimp metabolic respiration

* CO2decomposition\= organic matter decomposition.

---

**CO₂ Emission from Energy Consumption**

CO2energy=E×EF

Where:

* E\= energy consumed (kWh)

* EF\= emission factor (kg CO₂ per kWh).

---

**CO₂ Emission from Feed Production**

CO2feed=F×EFf

Where:

* F\= feed consumption (kg)

* EFf\= emission factor of feed production.

---

**CO₂ from Shrimp Respiration**

CO2respiration=B×R

Where:

* B\= shrimp biomass

* R\= respiration rate.

Csequestration=BsCc
